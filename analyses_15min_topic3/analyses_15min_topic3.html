Topic 3: Do early phosphosite changes persist after prolonged PASN exposure?

1. Question

Do the three phosphosites that show significant changes after 15 minutes of Pseudomonas aeruginosa supernatant (PASN) exposure in Staphylococcus aureus JE2 maintain similar changes after approximately 15 hours, and how do these changes relate to the abundance of their corresponding proteins?

4. Methods

We analysed three phosphosites that showed significant changes after 15 minutes of PASN exposure in S. aureus JE2 (anc): B7H15_01690 S33, B7H15_05655 T21 and IsaA S194.

Using the course-provided phosphoproteomics and total proteomics data, we compared TSB with 15-minute PASN exposure (SNpulse), TSB with approximately 15-hour exposure (SN), and SNpulse directly with SN.

We examined normalised abundances across three biological replicates per condition and compared phosphosite changes with the corresponding protein abundances. Results were classified as changed when detected in at least 2/3 replicates in both conditions, based on observed estimates, and meeting FDR < 0.05 and |log2FC| > 1.

Analyses and visualisations were performed in R using the course-provided functions and tidyverse packages.

S33 showed a sustained increase in phosphosite abundance after both exposure durations, whereas T21 and IsaA S194 showed strong changes only after 15 minutes. The phosphosite changes were generally larger than the corresponding protein changes. S33 remained strongly elevated at both exposure durations, while T21 showed a predominantly early response. The early increase in IsaA S194 coincided with increased IsaA protein abundance, suggesting that total protein abundance may contribute to its phosphosite signal.

6. Caveats

  1. Limited data: Only three previously identified phosphosites were analysed, with three biological replicates per condition. IsaA S194 had missing measurements, which may affect the precision of the results.

  2. Phosphosite vs protein abundance: Changes in phosphosite abundance may partly reflect changes in total protein abundance, as observed for IsaA S194. Phosphorylation occupancy and protein activity cannot be determined directly.

  3. Statistical interpretation: We used FDR < 0.05 and |log2FC| > 1 to classify changes. Results not meeting these criteria do not necessarily indicate the absence of a biological effect. Because the sites were selected based on their early changes, this is an exploratory analysis rather than independent validation.

  4. Exposure duration: Only two PASN exposure durations were compared (15 min and ~15 h). Therefore, we cannot determine when the changes occurred or exclude effects of other experimental differences.

7. Conclusion and next steps

The three phosphosites showed different responses to prolonged PASN exposure. 5’-nucleotidase S33 remained elevated after both 15 minutes and ~15 hours, whereas B7H15_05655 T21 and IsaA S194 showed strong early changes that were not maintained after ~15 hours.

The early increase in IsaA S194 coincided with increased total IsaA protein abundance, while the S33 phosphosite response was substantially larger than the corresponding protein change. This suggests that phosphosite abundance changes cannot always be explained by total protein abundance alone.

Overall, the results indicate both sustained and transient-like responses to PASN exposure. Additional time points and further investigation of the selected proteins could help clarify the underlying mechanisms.
